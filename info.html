How every Short is made

Curiosity Byte is an insight channel, not a news channel. Each video shows how power really works — spy agencies, money, big companies, powerful families — and tells the harsh reality, good and bad, so viewers keep a piece of hidden but useful knowledge.

“Every video: a famous name, one shocking true detail, the full story, and an honest ending that tells you what it means for you.”

From idea to upload

Research

Every night an AI script writer scans the last 72 hours of major news (Reuters, AP, BBC, Bloomberg, The Hindu, Indian Express and more) and looks for old files people are searching for right now — anniversaries, new films or series, newly declassified records. A backup writer takes over if the queue runs low.

Pick only strong topics

Each idea must pass four tests or it is dropped: Famous (a name viewers know), Shock (one true “wait, what?” detail), Stakes (money, power, war or lives) and You (a clear link to the viewer’s own life).

Write the script

A hook of 9 words or less, then a complete story in 85–105 words — setup, key moment, outcome — with a new fact every few seconds. The ending is written fresh every time: the harsh reality, who came out ahead and who carried the cost, and what the viewer can now notice in real life.

Fact-check every line

Every name, number and date is checked against the original sources; a claim counts only when two independent reliable sources agree. A rules check blocks unsafe wording, unlabelled theories and template endings before anything is made.

Voice and captions

A natural AI narrator reads the script; every word is timed so the bold captions pop in exactly as it is spoken, with numbers and years highlighted.

Footage that fits

Clips come from Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit (free licence only) and public-domain archive films. Every clip is scored against the whole script and must really match the story; a clip is never used in two videos.

Edit

Each video gets its own look: rotating caption styles, five file-card designs, a progress bar, quiet dark background music from a hand-picked library, and a spoken like-share-subscribe ending with animated buttons.

SEO

Keyword research from YouTube’s own search suggestions, a title with the famous name first and a topic emoji, a description with sources, tags, English subtitles, Hindi/Spanish/Portuguese translations, a discussion question as the first comment, and a thumbnail that shows the hook.

Publish and learn

Three Shorts a day go public at fixed times, made a day ahead so nothing is missed. Every morning the channel’s numbers are read back, and the script writers use them to choose better topics.

The mix

  • About 1 in 3 videos is a trending story from the last few days, linked to a documented past case.
  • The rest are evergreen hidden-history stories people still search for.
  • At least half are India stories or show India’s angle.
  • About 1 in 5 is a clearly labelled theory, always with the official explanation.

Rules the bot never breaks

Truth and safety

  • Only facts backed by official reports, courts, declassified documents or major outlets.
  • Theories are always labelled as theories, with the official explanation.
  • Private individuals are never accused; living public figures only as courts or inquiries found (“alleged”, “charged”).
  • No election claims in news stories, no medical misinformation, no conspiracy tropes about groups.

Footage and music

  • Only free-licence stock (Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit Free License) and public-domain archive films.
  • Mixkit clips marked “Restricted” are never used.
  • Music: hand-picked dark Mixkit tracks under the Stock Music Free License; a track is not repeated within 10 videos.
  • Documents, maps and stamps in the videos are the bot’s own drawings (recreations).

Behind the scenes

Fully in the cloud

Everything runs on GitHub Actions and Google Drive — research, video making, uploading and these numbers. Nothing runs on a personal computer.

Quality checks

Every video is checked for blank frames before upload; a failed step never uploads a broken video — the script simply waits for the next run.

This dashboard

Newest videos’ views refresh every hour from YouTube’s public feed. Full numbers, history and analytics update after every bot run and each morning. Upcoming topics stay private; only counts and times are shown.
